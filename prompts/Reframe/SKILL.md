---
name: reframe
description: change the coordinate system of a target so different structure becomes obvious. same object, new lens. representation transformation, not analogy or summary.
---

## tl;dr

reframe is NOT "say it differently." it's a menu of **representation shifts**:

**what you're trying to do determines which reframe move to use:**

- **see hidden structure** → factorization, constraint/flow view
- **escape stuck narratives** → axis shift, narrative flip
- **clarify incentives / power** → game-theoretic, market, institutional lenses
- **make it legible / measurable** → state–space, control, resource accounting
- **get out of your own head** → perspectival, phenomenological, somatic lenses

**the move this skill teaches as default**: COORDINATE SHIFT (same object, mapped into a specific lens with its own primitives)

- preserves the same underlying situation
- swaps out the descriptive grammar and objects (players, flows, states, constraints, arcs, etc.)
- surfaces new invariants, gradients, and failure modes
- changes which downstream operations are trivial vs impossible

this is ONE powerful move, but not the only way to reframe. see full taxonomy below.

**critical upstream**: identify TARGET + PURPOSE first, then pick reframe type and lens.

-----

## reframe types (full menu)

different reframes exert different kinds of pressure. pick based on what you're trying to accomplish.

### **axis shift** (orthogonalization)

**what**: change the main question / axis of evaluation, keep the situation.  
**when**: you're frame-locked on "is it good/bad" or "yes/no" and need a different dimension.  
**example**: "alpha vs public" → not 'which is better school' but 'which mechanism generates better signals about rune's type?'

---

### **game-theoretic reframe**

**what**: recast as a game: players, actions, information sets, payoffs, equilibria, deviations.  
**when**: incentives, strategic behavior, or second-order effects feel important but vague.  
**example**: "performance review" → not 'truth-telling ceremony' but 'cheap-talk game with career payoffs and signaling constraints.'

---

### **control-theoretic / dynamical reframe**

**what**: represent as state, inputs, outputs, dynamics, feedback loops, stability/instability.  
**when**: the issue is drift, oscillation, overcorrection, or 'i keep ending up here again.'  
**example**: "job choice paralysis" → not 'pick the best job' but 'design a stabilizing controller for identity under noise.'

---

### **market / mechanism-design reframe**

**what**: treat as market/auction/mechanism: agents, private info, rules, allocations, incentives, efficiency, gaming.  
**when**: allocation, fairness, or strategic manipulation matter.  
**example**: "school admissions" → not 'is alpha good?' but 'what mechanism (lottery, screening, bidding) are we entering and what does it optimize?'

---

### **factorization / decomposition reframe**

**what**: break object into dimensions, modules, or factors; represent as vector of components, not monolith.  
**when**: everything feels tangled and 'one big thing.'  
**example**: "job a vs job b" → disentangle money, learning, status, autonomy, commute, childcare implications.

---

### **resource / accounting reframe**

**what**: recast in terms of scarce resources and budgets (time, attention, energy, optionality, risk capital, emotional bandwidth).  
**when**: tradeoffs are implicit, or you're 'overspending' invisibly.  
**example**: "side project" → not 'fun vs serious' but 'where do i allocate my sharpest 10 hours/week of cognition?'

---

### **narrative / dramaturgical reframe**

**what**: treat as story: characters, arcs, conflicts, stakes, acts, payoffs, audience.  
**when**: self-concept, identity, or social meaning dominate behavior.  
**example**: "staying on garden leave" → not 'rational option value' but 'am i in a stagnation arc or a chrysalis arc?'

---

### **phenomenological / experiential reframe**

**what**: focus on how the situation is *felt* from the inside: sensations, affect, temporality, texture.  
**when**: lived experience diverges from abstract model; something 'feels off.'  
**example**: "garden leave" → not 'preference revelation' but 'how does time dilation, boredom, guilt, and relief actually feel day to day?'

---

### **somatic / embodied reframe**

**what**: treat it as a body pattern: tension, orientation, activation, freeze/flight/fight, posture, breath.  
**when**: chronic patterns (pain, anxiety, avoidance) dominate but stay conceptual.  
**example**: "migraine pathway" → not 'neurological fluke' but 'guarded pattern of tissue trying to protect something.'

---

### **computational / algorithmic reframe**

**what**: represent as algorithm: inputs, outputs, complexity, data needs, failure modes, scalability.  
**when**: you suspect intractability, hidden assumptions, or algorithmic shortcuts.  
**example**: "how i make career decisions" → not 'i just feel it out' but 'here's the actual algorithm i seem to be running.'

---

### **institutional / coordination reframe**

**what**: treat as institution: roles, norms, rules-of-thumb, enforcement, failure modes, coalitions.  
**when**: group behavior looks irrational but persists.  
**example**: "how a team makes tech choices" → not 'picking the best library' but 'office politics around ownership and status-laden tech.'

---

### **perspectival / agent reframe**

**what**: adopt a specific agent's world model and values; describe situation from inside their head.  
**when**: conflict or negotiation is central; empathy deficit.  
**example**: "job a vs job b" from alyson's view → different weighting of risk, time, childcare, emotional load.

---

### **foil / contrast reframe**

**what**: define the thing by its contrast class; specify what it is *not* to clarify what it is.  
**when**: category boundaries are fuzzy; a concept feels bloated.  
**example**: "this is a *ritual* not a *process*; rituals are for identity + coordination, processes for throughput + reliability."

-----

## picking the right reframe type

**fast heuristics by purpose:**

| your goal                             | use these moves                                                  |
|--------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| see hidden structure                 | factorization, resource, control-theoretic                       |
| escape being stuck / looping         | axis shift, narrative, computational                             |
| understand incentives / power        | game-theoretic, market, institutional                            |
| make it measurable / engineerable    | state–space/control, resource/accounting, computational          |
| align with lived experience          | phenomenological, somatic, perspectival                          |
| prepare for negotiation / buy-in     | perspectival, game-theoretic, narrative                          |
| detect category/type errors          | foil/contrast, ontological / type-safety                         |

**generators** (concrete ways to mint reframes):

- swap **unit of analysis**: individual ↔ dyad ↔ org ↔ market ↔ ecosystem  
- swap **objective**: performance ↔ robustness ↔ fairness ↔ legibility ↔ optionality  
- swap **time lens**: moment ↔ week ↔ year ↔ decade ↔ lifetime  
- swap **primary resource**: money ↔ time ↔ attention ↔ social capital ↔ physiological load  
- swap **formalism**: story ↔ game ↔ dynamical system ↔ market ↔ algorithm ↔ ritual

## signature

reframe(target, lens) → representation_in_new_coords

	•	target: the situation/problem/belief/conflict/plan you care about.
	•	lens: the representation/coordinate system you want to view it through (from the menu above, or custom).

contract: same underlying object, different coordinates. if the ‘reframed’ version would surprise someone who knows the actual situation well, it’s allowed only because of new structure, not hallucinated facts.

⸻

process (step by step)

step 0: clarify target + aim
	•	target: write 1–3 sentences that capture the object of interest (no digressions, no solutions).
	•	aim: what do you want out of the reframe? e.g.:
	•	find new levers
	•	understand why you’re stuck
	•	make it legible for a specific stakeholder
	•	translate into engineering / experimental form
	•	align map with felt sense

step 1: pick reframe type + lens
	•	choose reframe type from the menu (axis shift, game-theoretic, etc.)
	•	instantiate a specific lens:
	•	game-theoretic → “2-player repeated game, incomplete info”
	•	control-theoretic → “state with noisy observations and delayed feedback”
	•	market → “two-sided market with asymmetric information,” etc.

if in doubt:
	•	pick one structural lens (game, control, market, algorithm)
	•	and/or one experiential lens (phenomenological, somatic, perspectival)

step 2: extract core structure of target

strip away prose; identify primitives in lens-agnostic form:
	•	who/what are the entities?
	•	what are their goals/constraints?
	•	what changes over time?
	•	what are the key bottlenecks, uncertainties, irreversibilities?

keep it short and structural.

step 3: instantiate lens schema

for the chosen lens, explicitly list its native primitives. for example:
	•	game-theoretic:
	•	players, strategies, information sets, payoffs, equilibria, deviations
	•	control-theoretic:
	•	state, inputs, outputs, dynamics, noise, controller, stability
	•	market/mechanism:
	•	agents, private info, rules, allocation, prices, externalities
	•	resource:
	•	resources, budgets, inflows/outflows, constraints, exchange rates
	•	narrative:
	•	protagonist, antagonistic forces, stakes, acts, turning points
	•	somatic:
	•	regions, tension/relaxation, orientation, threshold, cycles

this step prevents “metaphor slop”: if you can’t enumerate lens primitives, you don’t have a real lens.

step 4: map target → lens

create the mapping:
	•	assign each relevant element of the target to lens primitives
	•	make explicit:
	•	who are the players / resources / states?
	•	what are the transitions / feedback loops / payoffs?
	•	what corresponds to ‘winning,’ ‘failure,’ ‘stability,’ ‘runaway’?

this is where the coordinate change actually happens.

step 5: surface what becomes obvious

now ask, inside the lens:
	•	what invariants pop out? (conserved quantities, stable patterns)
	•	what gradients appear? (directions everything tends to move)
	•	what failure modes are now obvious?
	•	what levers exist that weren’t visible in original frame?
	•	what becomes trivial that was hard before?

this is the whole point of reframing. if nothing new becomes obvious, you picked the wrong lens or mapped sloppily.

step 6: return the reframe as standalone description

write the reframe so it:
	•	can be read without seeing the original prose
	•	clearly identifies:
	•	the key objects, in lens terms
	•	the dynamics/structure revealed by the lens
	•	at least one fresh implication for decisions / next steps

do not just summarize or paraphrase; the representation must feel fundamentally different.

⸻

quality criteria

representation preservation
	•	same underlying situation; no invented entities/events
	•	a domain expert would recognize their problem, just spoken in a different formalism
	•	differences are in description and emphasis, not in raw facts

lens fidelity
	•	lens primitives are used correctly (a ‘state’ is actually a state, not a vibe)
	•	no mixed metaphors unless explicitly compositional
	•	an expert in that formalism would not roll their eyes

salience shift
	•	reframe exposes at least one new invariant / gradient / failure mode
	•	reframe suggests new levers or constraints that were invisible before
	•	downstream operations (attack, operationalize, prioritize) become easier / clearer

coherence
	•	internally consistent (no contradictions inside reframed view)
	•	no rhetorical fluff justified only by style
	•	concrete enough that someone could act differently because of it

⸻

genre-specific patterns

the shape of a good reframe depends on what the target is.

decision doc / tradeoff

target: “should i take job a or job b?”
useful lenses:
	•	factorization (dimensions)
	•	resource (budgets)
	•	game-theoretic (bargaining power over time)
	•	narrative (life arc)

reframe structure:
	•	extract dimensions (money, status, learning, family, health, optionality)
	•	build resource table: how each job spends/earns each resource over time
	•	or: recast as bargaining game between you-now and you-in-5-years

⸻

relationship conflict

target: “alyson thinks alpha is a grift, i don’t.”
useful lenses:
	•	game-theoretic (payoffs, beliefs)
	•	perspectival (alyson’s model)
	•	phenomenological (how each regime feels)

reframe structure:
	•	represent as 2-player game with different payoff matrices over dimensions like safety, effort, social signal, child welfare
	•	or: narrate from alyson’s perspective, what she experiences as risk/cost

⸻

chronic somatic issue

target: “migraine along trigeminal, triggered easily.”
useful lenses:
	•	somatic
	•	control (nervous system as controller)
	•	resource (buffer capacity, recovery time)

reframe structure:
	•	describe as a protective pattern that’s overshooting; identify feedback loops and thresholds
	•	identify levers: where to widen window, where to reduce noise or load

⸻

strategy / architecture

target: “architecture for reasoning tools / skills / ft.”
useful lenses:
	•	computational (algorithms, complexity)
	•	market (ecosystem, complementarity)
	•	control (humans as supervisors of agents)

reframe structure:
	•	represent as algorithm stack (primitives, compositors, evaluators)
	•	or: as market of skills, where stability + interoperability are scarce capital

⸻

anti-patterns (common failures)

analogy instead of reframe

symptom: “it’s like X” stories that don’t change the formal structure.
fix: require explicit mapping into lens primitives; analogy can be input to reframe, not the output.

⸻

summary instead of reframe

symptom: shorter restatement, same ontology.
fix: ask “what object types changed?” if all types stayed identical, it’s not a reframe.

⸻

hallucinated constraints

symptom: new ‘objects’ and ‘limits’ appear that don’t track any known part of the real situation.
fix: tie every structural element in the lens back to something in the original; if you can’t, mark it explicitly as hypothetical.

⸻

vibe-lens

symptom: lens is just a mood (“reframe in a hopeful light”) not a formalism or schema.
fix: insist on lenses with explicit primitives and rules (game, market, state-space, narrative, etc.).

⸻

integration with other operations

upstream:
	•	decompose: helps extract dimensions/components before reframing.
	•	framestorm (if you build it): generates candidate lenses when you’re stuck.

downstream:
	•	attack: once reframed, you can attack failure modes more structurally (e.g. control-instability, incentive-misalignment).
	•	test: push the reframed system to extremes (n → ∞, resource → 0, etc.).
	•	operationalize: define metrics / observables in the new coordinates.
	•	prioritize: rank options using the resource or game-theoretic representation.
	•	integrate: reconcile original frame and reframed version, or integrate multiple reframes.

parallel:
	•	perspectivize: agent-specific reframes; often combined: reframe-as-game + perspectivize-per-player.
	•	antithesize: reframe is often a precursor; changing coordinates makes better antithesis easier.

⸻

examples (mini, to illustrate diversity)

example 1: career choice → control system

target: “i can’t decide between high-paid but stressful job a and interesting but risky job b.”

reframe(control):
	•	state = (health, optionality, learning, relationship_stability)
	•	inputs = (job_choice, hours, recovery practices)
	•	controller = your decision policy + coping mechanisms
	•	goal = keep state in ‘good enough’ region while nudging trend up in learning/optionality
	•	implication: best job is the one your current controller can actually stabilize, not the one with max nominal payoff.

⸻

example 2: school decision → mechanism design

target: “pick alpha vs public for child.”

reframe(mechanism_design):
	•	agents: parents, child, school operators, colleges, employers
	•	mechanism: who chooses whom, based on what signals, with what feedback
	•	alpha: high-signal, high-noise new mechanism with sharp selection, strong sales, unclear long-run equilibria
	•	public: low-signal, low-variance mechanism embedded in a bigger institutional game
	•	implication: what you’re choosing is signal generation regime and selection environment, not ‘curriculum quality’ per se.

⸻

example 3: migraines → somatic pattern

target: wife has migraines triggered by small deviations.”

reframe(somatic/control):
	•	system = her nervous system as adaptive controller
	•	pattern = over-fit protective reflex along trigeminal pathway; threshold too low
	•	levers = raise threshold, increase buffer capacity, give nervous system safe counter-evidence slowly
	•	implication: interventions should feel like renegotiating protection, not ‘fighting symptoms.’

⸻

meta-note

reframe is REPRESENTATIONAL MAINTENANCE. most reasoning errors are less “bad logic” and more “optimizing in the wrong coordinates.”

this skill:
	•	makes the coordinate system explicit
	•	lets you swap it deliberately
	•	turns “i feel stuck” into “i chose a bad basis; let’s change it”

the goal isn’t endless reframing as an aesthetic—the goal is to land in a representation where the next move (attack, test, operationalize, decide) is obvious and cheap.
